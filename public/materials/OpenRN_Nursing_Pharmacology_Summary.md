# Open RN Nursing Pharmacology (2nd Edition) - Clinical Study Guide
Source: Open RN / NCBI Bookshelf (NBK595000) - Open Educational Resource

## 1. Pharmacokinetics & Pharmacodynamics
- **Pharmacokinetics (ADME)**:
  - **Absorption**: Movement of drug from administration site to bloodstream (bioavailability).
  - **Distribution**: Movement of drug to tissues and site of action (protein binding, blood-brain barrier).
  - **Metabolism**: Biotransformation primarily in the Liver (cytochrome P450 system, first-pass effect).
  - **Excretion**: Removal of drug primarily via Kidneys (BUN, Serum Creatinine, GFR), bile, or lungs.
- **Half-Life (t1/2)**: Time required for serum drug concentration to decrease by 50% (takes ~4-5 half-lives to reach steady state or eliminate).
- **Peak and Trough Levels**:
  - Trough: Drawn immediately (30 min) BEFORE the next dose (evaluates toxicity risk).
  - Peak: Drawn at peak drug concentration (evaluates therapeutic efficacy).

## 2. High-Alert Medications & Safety
- **High-Alert Acronym (PINCH)**:
  - **P** - Potassium IV (Never give IV push; lethal cardiac arrest)
  - **I** - Insulin (Double check with 2nd RN)
  - **N** - Narcotics / Opioids (Monitor RR, sedation scale, keep Naloxone/Narcan available)
  - **C** - Chemotherapeutic agents (Special handling and double verification)
  - **H** - Heparin & Anticoagulants (Check aPTT/INR/anti-Xa, check for bleeding)
- **10 Rights of Medication Administration**: Right Patient, Drug, Dose, Route, Time, Documentation, Reason, Response, Education, Right to Refuse.

## 3. Key Drug Classes & Antidotes
- **Opioids** -> Antidote: *Naloxone (Narcan)*
- **Acetaminophen (Paracetamol)** -> Antidote: *N-acetylcysteine (NAC / Acetadote)*
- **Heparin** -> Antidote: *Protamine Sulfate*
- **Warfarin (Coumadin)** -> Antidote: *Vitamin K (Phytonadione)*
- **Digoxin** -> Antidote: *Digoxin Immune Fab (DigiFab)* (Therapeutic Digoxin level: 0.5 – 2.0 ng/mL; watch for hypokalemia predisposing to toxicity).
- **Beta-Blockers (-lol)**: Hold if Heart Rate < 60 bpm or Systolic BP < 100 mmHg.
- **ACE Inhibitors (-pril)**: Risk of dry cough, hyperkalemia, and angioedema.
